# Ottawa's Oct. 29, 2026 PCO National Security Briefing: Admiralty-Graded Assessment

*Pre-announcement assessment, prepared the evening of October 28, 2026, before the 9 a.m. ET embargo lifted. Probabilities are analytic judgments, not statistical estimates. Admiralty grades: source reliability A–F, information credibility 1–6.*

**Bottom line:** The PCO briefing is most likely the release of Canada's first national security strategy since *Securing an Open Society* (April 2004). Assessed **likely to highly likely (~80%)**. It would probably be presented as a whole-of-society strategy centred on economic security and "strategic autonomy," possibly with first concrete steps such as security screening fixes. No post-embargo publication, leak or reporter post confirming the content was found at time of writing.

## Key findings

1. **The format points to PCO's own policy file.** The advisory came from PCO, not Public Safety or DND: background only, attributable to senior officials, embargo to 9 a.m. ET, materials in advance. PCO used the same format for the Canada–Alberta MOU (May 15, 2026) and the West Coast Oil Pipeline update (Oct. 1, 2026), where the embargo lifted when the PM began speaking. Here the embargo lifts at a fixed clock time with no matching PM event, suggesting a document release rather than a staged PM announcement. The NSS sits in PCO under the Senior Diplomatic and International Affairs Advisor, who is also the NSIA.
2. **Timing matches David Morrison's public signal.** CP (Jim Bronskill, Oct. 23) reported Morrison told SECU, in French, he hoped the strategy would be public "within a week or two." PCO spokesperson Mélany Gauvin said it would be whole-of-society, increase focus on economic security, and chart Canada's strategic autonomy.
3. **Expert community primed for release this week.** CIGI (Oct. 27) said the government was planning to publish soon; Wesley Wark (Oct. 28) called it imminent. Neither shows inside knowledge of contents.
4. **Correction:** the NSIA position was not ultimately eliminated. PCO said in March 2026 that Morrison would also be NSIA; Dominic Rochon became Deputy Secretary to the Cabinet (NS&I).
5. **Screening reform is a plausible early deliverable**, given the NATO intern espionage case and Morrison's support for a government-wide clearance registry.
6. **An F-35 decision is a poor fit.** RCAF accepted its first two F-35As on Oct. 22, 2026; review remains open-ended; a fighter decision would come from DND/PSPC, not a PCO background briefing.
7. **Base fires: coincidence, not connection.** DND said the Oct. 17 CFB Wainwright fire showed no indication of terrorist activity. No Oct. 28 Gagetown fire was found in reporting at time of writing.
8. **Bill C-40** (first reading Oct. 6) makes a new defence-industrial bill redundant. Budget is Nov. 5, 2026; funding may be held for it.
